# Reviewer 3 Report

> **Reviewer Focus**: Clarity & Completeness
> **Paper**: Test-Time Computing for Spatio-Temporal Weather Forecasting with Foundation Models
> **Target Venue**: Scopus-indexed applied machine learning / information sciences journal (master's-thesis paper)
> **Review Date**: 2026-11-07

## Summary
The paper proposes Spatial-Cluster Best-of-$N$, a training-free test-time-computing (TTC) framework that scales inference-time compute for a frozen time-series foundation model (chiefly Chronos-2) applied to spatio-temporal weather forecasting. The method generates full-resolution "fine" candidates and coarse, cluster-aggregated "anchor" candidates from the same frozen backbone, prunes the fine candidates with a soft advection/continuity physics filter, and selects a final forecast using a composite verifier that compares trend (DTW), seasonal frequency (FFT), and spatial-roughness (graph Laplacian) consistency against the coarse anchors. The paper states it evaluates on two datasets, ERA5 (gridded reanalysis) and WEATHER-5K (station network), with an extensive planned ablation suite (verifier components, compute-budget scaling, backbone generality, clustering strategy, budget allocation, verifier-weight sensitivity).

## Overall Assessment
Setting aside the placeholder numbers (as instructed), the manuscript has a coherent and reasonably well-motivated core method, a logical top-level structure, and an ablation plan that is unusually thorough for a thesis-stage paper. However, the draft has a substantial number of internal-consistency and completeness problems that are independent of the missing results: a second dataset (WEATHER-5K) is introduced in the Problem Definition and Experimental Settings but never appears in a single table, figure, or results discussion, and is explicitly contradicted by the Conclusion's claim that the work is "confined to a single reanalysis dataset (ERA5)"; the method's own name is inconsistently given as both "SC-BoN" and "ST-TTC" throughout the paper, with "ST-TTC" also being the exact name the paper assigns to the directly-competing prior work it is trying to differentiate from; six citation keys used in the text have no corresponding entry in the bibliography file; and several of the method's core scoring/filtering terms and default hyperparameters are described only in prose, without equations or concrete values even as placeholders. These are not "missing numbers" issues — they are structural and editorial problems that materially impede a reader's ability to understand, trust, and reproduce the work, and they should be resolved before the manuscript advances to a results-complete version.

## Strengths
1. **Clear, redundant presentation of the overall pipeline**: Section 4.1 pairs a prose walk-through, a TikZ architecture figure (Figure "fig:arch"), and a compact stage/input/output table (Table "tab:overview") that all tell the same four-stage story consistently. This kind of triple redundancy (text + diagram + table) is good practice and makes the high-level idea easy to grasp quickly.
2. **Honest, non-overclaiming limitations discussion**: The Conclusion explicitly flags that the physics term is a soft filter rather than a hard constraint, that the clustering is static and not adapted to the forecast window, and that accuracy trades off against compute. This kind of self-critical framing is exactly what a reviewer wants to see, and it is well-written when considered on its own (independent of the dataset-scope contradiction noted below).
3. **Thorough, well-reasoned ablation design**: The plan to isolate each verifier term, the physics filter, the compute budget, the backbone choice, the clustering strategy, the fine/coarse budget split, and the verifier weight ratio (Sections 5.3–5.5) is unusually comprehensive, and each study's text clearly states what exactly is being isolated and why (e.g., "because both reference points draw from the same candidate budget and the same frozen backbone, this gap isolates the contribution of the three-term verifier..."). This shows real methodological maturity even before the numbers are filled in.
4. **Internally consistent default hyperparameter choice**: The default fine/coarse split used in the main experiments ($N_A{=}15$, $N_C{=}5$, i.e., 75/25) matches the split that the budget-allocation analysis (Section 5.5) reports as empirically best among {50/50, 75/25, 90/10}. This is a small but genuine point of coherence that suggests the design choices are not arbitrary.

## Weaknesses

1. **A second dataset is introduced and then abandoned (Major)**: The Problem Definition (Section 3.1) and Experimental Settings (Section 5.1) explicitly describe two evaluation datasets — ERA5 and WEATHER-5K, complete with its own history/horizon setting ($L_H{=}48$, $L_F{=}24$) and variable set (temperature, dewpoint, wind direction, wind speed, sea-level pressure). Yet every single results table and figure (`tab:main_results`, `tab:aggregate`, `tab:search_strategies`, `tab:ablation_verifier`, `tab:ablation_compute`, `tab:ablation_backbones`, `tab:ablation_clustering`, `tab:budget_split`, `tab:w3_sweep`, `fig:scaling`) is explicitly scoped to ERA5 only, and the Conclusion flatly states "our evaluation is confined to a single reanalysis dataset (ERA5)... so whether the reported gains transfer to other datasets, resolutions, or variable sets remains to be verified" — directly contradicting the earlier two-dataset framing. Tellingly, the ablation subsections are labeled (Q1), (Q2), (Q3), (Q5) — Q4 is missing entirely, which strongly suggests a cross-dataset generality study was planned (and perhaps the natural place for WEATHER-5K results) but dropped without reconciling the surrounding text.
   - *Suggestion*: Either (a) add WEATHER-5K results as the missing "Q4" generality study and update the Conclusion accordingly, or (b) demote WEATHER-5K to "future work" everywhere (Problem Definition, Experimental Settings, Abstract framing) so the paper's scope is stated consistently as ERA5-only at this draft stage.

2. **The method's own name is inconsistent, and collides with the name of the paper's main differentiator in Related Work (Major)**: The Introduction's contribution statement, the paper-organization sentence, the architecture figure caption, the overview table caption, and one row of Table `tab:search_strategies` ("Ours (SC-BoN, two-scale)") call the method **SC-BoN**. However, the Abstract, and essentially the entire Results/Ablation/Conclusion text (e.g., "...gains of ST-TTC come from...", "...re-running the full ST-TTC pipeline...", "ST-TTC yields a positive relative RMSE reduction...", and the Conclusion's "We proposed Spatial-Cluster Best-of-$N$ (ST-TTC)...") call the identical method **ST-TTC**. Crucially, "ST-TTC" is also the exact abbreviation the paper itself assigns to Chen and Liang's prior work in Related Work ("Chen and Liang... recently introduced ST-TTC, an inference-stage calibration framework..."), which the Introduction spends a full paragraph distinguishing the present work from. A reader cannot tell, from the acronym alone, whether a given sentence in Sections 5–6 is describing the proposed method or the competing baseline it is being contrasted against.
   - *Suggestion*: Pick one name for the proposed method (SC-BoN is the safer choice, since ST-TTC is already claimed by prior work) and do a global replace. If "ST-TTC" must be kept for the proposed method for thesis/branding reasons, rename Chen and Liang's method to something else on first use (e.g., "the calibration-based ST-TTC of Chen and Liang") every time it recurs, to avoid ambiguity.

3. **Six citation keys used in-text have no corresponding entry in `references.bib` (Major)**: `han2024weather5k` (cited for the WEATHER-5K dataset), `wang2025fredf` (frequency-domain forecasting objective), `shuman2013gsp` (graph signal processing / Dirichlet energy), `scheuerer2015variogram` (variogram score), `deboer2005cem` (cross-entropy method), and `eriksson2019turbo` (TuRBO-1) are all cited with `\citep{...}` in the manuscript but are absent from `paper/references.bib` (verified by direct inspection — no matching BibTeX entries exist for any of these six keys). As written, these citations would render as broken references ("?") in the compiled PDF. This is unrelated to the placeholder-numbers caveat and should be fixed regardless of experimental status.
   - *Suggestion*: Add the six missing BibTeX entries (all six are identifiable, published works — e.g., Han et al.'s WEATHER-5K paper, Wang et al.'s FreDF, Shuman et al.'s graph signal processing survey, Scheuerer & Hamill's variogram score paper, de Boer et al.'s CEM tutorial, Eriksson et al.'s TuRBO NeurIPS paper) before the next revision.

4. **Core scoring/filtering terms are described only in prose, with no explicit equations, and several default hyperparameters are never given even as placeholders (Major)**: Section 4.3 (physics filter) and Section 4.4 (composite verifier) describe $\mathcal{R}_{\text{phys}}$, the activity penalty, $E_{\text{trend}}$, $E_{\text{FFT}}$, and $E_{\text{spatial}}$ entirely in words; only the final linear combination (Eq. 3) and the arg-min selection rule (Eq. 4) are actually written as equations. Given that this scoring machinery is the paper's central technical contribution, the absence of explicit formulas for each component term is a significant reproducibility gap. Separately, several hyperparameters that a reader would need to reproduce the main results are never stated anywhere, not even as a bracketed placeholder: the kNN graph size $k$ and Gaussian bandwidth $\sigma$ (Section 4.2); the default number of clusters $M$ used for the main results (the only numeric value, $M{=}32$, appears inside the later budget-allocation ablation in Section 5.5, not in Section 5.1's settings, and is never flagged as "the default"); and the default $K_A$ survivors out of $N_A{=}15$ (again only given as $K_A{=}5$ inside the Section 5.5 ablation). The default weight ratio $w_1{:}w_2{:}w_3$ is also never stated.
   - *Suggestion*: Add explicit equations for $\mathcal{R}_{\text{phys}}$, $E_{\text{trend}}$, $E_{\text{FFT}}$, and $E_{\text{spatial}}$, and consolidate the full default hyperparameter set ($k$, $\sigma$, $M$, $K_A$, $w_1{:}w_2{:}w_3$) into one place in Section 5.1 (a settings table would work well), even if the exact tuned values are still placeholders at this stage.

5. **Key reported metrics are never formally defined (Major/Minor borderline)**: The Metrics paragraph (Section 5.1) names Moran's $I$, latitude-weighted RMSE, and ACC but never defines what any of them compute. Moran's $I$ is glossed only once, in a table caption, as "(higher is better)" — with no indication of its range, formula, or what "spatial coherence" concretely means in this context. The latitude weighting behind RMSE$_{\mathrm{lw}}$ is never given a formula. "ACC" is used as an acronym without first being spelled out as "anomaly correlation coefficient" anywhere in the text. A reader outside spatial statistics or climate science cannot evaluate claims that hinge on these metrics (e.g., "the clearest advantage on spatial coherence" in the Main Results paragraph) without consulting outside sources.
   - *Suggestion*: Add one sentence per metric with either an equation or a precise citation plus a one-line definition of what high/low values mean physically.

6. **Table content does not match what the accompanying text promises (Major)**: The budget-allocation paragraph (Section 5.5) states "we... report the resulting error **and spatial-coherence metrics** in Table `tab:budget_split`," but the table itself has only one error-type metric per split column (RMSE, per the caption) plus a runtime column — there is no spatial-coherence (e.g., Moran's $I$) column anywhere in the table.
   - *Suggestion*: Either add the promised spatial-coherence column(s) or correct the prose to say only RMSE and runtime are reported for this particular sweep.

7. **A "Step 2" table is presented with no visible "Step 1" (Minor)**: The verifier-weight-sensitivity paragraph (Section 5.5) describes a two-step tuning procedure: "the trend and frequency weights $w_1$ and $w_2$ are first balanced against each other" (implicitly Step 1), after which "the spatial weight is then swept" (Step 2). Table `tab:w3_sweep`'s caption literally begins "Step 2 — spatial weight $w_3$ sweep...", but Step 1's result, search range, or tuning criterion is never reported anywhere, not even as a placeholder.
   - *Suggestion*: Either show a Step-1 table/description (what values of $w_1, w_2$ were tried and by what criterion they were "balanced") or drop the "Step 2" framing and describe the $w_3$ sweep as a single, self-contained analysis at a fixed, stated $w_1{:}w_2$ ratio.

8. **Confusing near-duplicate "Best-of-$N$" baselines across two tables (Minor)**: Table `tab:main_results` includes "Best-of-$N$ (simple scorer)," defined as using a truth-free *median/consensus* scorer. Section 5.2's Table `tab:search_strategies` includes a differently-defined "Best-of-$N$ (sample)," which per the prose uses the authors' own *full composite verifier* (just applied to single-scale candidates). These serve two different ablation purposes (isolating the verifier's contribution vs. isolating the two-scale architecture's contribution) but their near-identical names invite readers to conflate them, and the text never explicitly cross-references the distinction.
   - *Suggestion*: Rename one of the two (e.g., "BoN–consensus" vs. "BoN–verifier, single-scale") and add a sentence in Section 5.2 explicitly noting how this baseline relates to the "Best-of-$N$ (simple scorer)" reference point used in the Main Results table.

9. **Ambiguous scope of the Main Results table (Minor)**: Section 5.1 states three history/horizon configurations are evaluated: $(512,64)$, $(512,128)$, $(1024,96)$. Table `tab:aggregate`'s caption explicitly says it is "averaged over the three variables and the three forecast settings," but Table `tab:main_results`'s caption only says "averaged over all grid points," with no statement of whether it covers one configuration or all three pooled together. The Forecast-settings paragraph itself only says metrics are "averaged over all locations," without clarifying whether this also means pooled across the three settings.
   - *Suggestion*: State explicitly, in both the relevant paragraph and the Table 1 caption, whether the main results are per-setting or pooled, and if pooled, note where (if anywhere) a per-setting breakdown can be found.

10. **Overclaim in the Introduction not matched by the honestly-described method (Minor)**: The second paragraph of the Introduction frames the research gap as the absence of a framework that scales test-time compute "while **strictly enforcing** spatial continuum coherence and atmospheric governing equations." Section 4.3 and the Conclusion, by contrast, correctly and explicitly describe the physics term as "only a soft filter" that "can only down-weight physically implausible candidates, not guarantee that the selected forecast satisfies the underlying conservation laws exactly." The word "strictly" in the motivating paragraph oversells what the method is later honestly shown to deliver.
    - *Suggestion*: Soften "strictly enforcing" in the Introduction (e.g., to "scoring consistency with" or "softly enforcing") to match the more careful framing already used in Section 4.3 and the Conclusion.

11. **Imprecise, inconsistent terminology for the spatial verifier term (Minor)**: The Introduction's contribution bullet describes the spatial term as "spatial smoothness via graph Laplacian **divergence**," while the Method (Section 4.4) correctly and more carefully defines it as "a graph-Laplacian smoothness functional, the normalized **Dirichlet energy**." Divergence (a first-order differential operator on vector fields) and Dirichlet energy (a quadratic smoothness functional) are not the same mathematical object; the Introduction's phrasing should be aligned with the Method's more precise description.
    - *Suggestion*: Replace "graph Laplacian divergence" with "graph-Laplacian (Dirichlet-energy) smoothness" in the Introduction's contribution bullet.

12. **Unclear how the physics filter handles WEATHER-5K's wind representation (Minor)**: Section 3.1 states that every node's variable set "always include[s] the temperature and the horizontal wind used by the physics term," but Section 5.1 describes WEATHER-5K's wind variables as "wind direction" and "wind speed," not orthogonal horizontal components (u, v) as used for ERA5 (u10, v10). The advection/continuity residual in Section 4.3 requires a vector wind field to compute transport and divergence; the paper never states whether/how direction+speed are converted into (u, v) form for WEATHER-5K.
    - *Suggestion*: Add a sentence clarifying the conversion (e.g., $u = -\text{speed}\cdot\sin(\text{direction})$, $v = -\text{speed}\cdot\cos(\text{direction})$) or clarify that the physics filter is currently ERA5-only, in which case Section 3.1's "always include" claim needs qualifying.

13. **Positioning tension between "continuous fields" framing and the station-network dataset (Minor)**: The Introduction differentiates the present work from Chen and Liang's ST-TTC partly on the grounds that the latter operates on "discrete sensor graphs, such as traffic and air-quality monitoring networks," while this paper targets "continuous physical spatio-temporal fields." WEATHER-5K, the paper's own second dataset, is itself a discrete, irregularly-spaced station network, structurally similar to the "sensor graphs" used to critique prior work. This blunts the sharpness of the stated differentiation once WEATHER-5K is in scope.
    - *Suggestion*: Either soften the "continuous fields vs. discrete sensor graphs" contrast, or add a sentence explaining why atmospheric station networks differ materially (e.g., in physical governing equations, spatial density, or variable semantics) from traffic/air-quality sensor graphs.

14. **No consolidated algorithm/pseudocode block (Minor)**: Given four interacting stages (clustering, coarse branch, fine branch + physics filter, verifier + selection) with notation spread across Sections 3–4, a single pseudocode box summarizing the full pipeline (inputs, outputs, loop structure) would materially help reproducibility; none is currently included.
    - *Suggestion*: Add an `algorithm` environment (the LaTeX template already imports `algorithm2e`) summarizing Sections 4.2–4.4 as a single procedure.

## Detailed Comments

### Abstract
- Introduces the method as "Spatial-Cluster Best-of-$N$ (ST-TTC)"; this abbreviation is inconsistent with the "SC-BoN" abbreviation introduced in the Introduction's contribution bullet (see Weakness 2).
- Reports results only for ERA5, consistent with the fact that no WEATHER-5K results appear anywhere in the paper — but this means the Abstract's claims implicitly scope the whole contribution to one dataset even though the body later claims two.

### Introduction
- The "three concrete research gaps" (physical plausibility, multi-scale spatial coupling, inference-budget efficiency) are clearly numbered and easy to follow, but gaps 1 and 3 both end up being addressed by the *same* single mechanism (the physics pre-filter), while the composite verifier (contribution 3) is not explicitly tied back to any one of the three numbered gaps. The one-to-one mapping between "gaps" and "contributions" that the structure implies is therefore only partially realized; a short explicit mapping sentence would help.
- The claim of "strictly enforcing... atmospheric governing equations" (second paragraph) is not consistent with the softly-filtered design described later (see Weakness 10).
- The contribution bullets describe the evaluation as covering only "multi-variable ERA5 reanalysis data," with no mention of WEATHER-5K, even though Section 5.1 introduces WEATHER-5K as a second dataset. This is the first of several places where the two-dataset claim and the text diverge (see Weakness 1).

### Related Work
- Well organized into five clearly delineated subsections with topic sentences that make the related-work taxonomy easy to follow.
- The distinction drawn between "test-time search/editing" and "test-time adaptation/online calibration" (Section 2.3) is clear and useful for positioning.
- The repeated use of "ST-TTC" to name Chen and Liang's prior work here is technically correct (it is the name the original paper uses) but sets up the naming collision flagged in Weakness 2 once the same string is reused for the authors' own method elsewhere.

### Problem Definition
- Section 3.1 is nicely generalized to cover both grid cells and weather stations uniformly via the graph abstraction ("a location is a grid cell when the data is a gridded reanalysis, or a weather station when the data is a station network... the framework treats $\mathcal{V}$ uniformly"). This is a clean piece of writing and a genuine strength at the formalism level.
- However, this generalization is not followed through into the Results (Weakness 1), making the careful dual-domain framing here feel unfulfilled by the end of the paper.
- Section 3.2's frozen-constraint equation (Eq. 1, $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{0}$) is clear and is referenced appropriately later (Section 3.2 prose, and implicitly throughout Section 4).

### Method
- Section 4.1 (Framework Overview) is clearly written and well-supported by Figure `fig:arch` and Table `tab:overview`.
- Section 4.2 (Spatial Clustering) explains *why* great-circle distance is used (pole convergence, irregular station proximity) — a nice piece of motivated writing — but never gives the numeric $k$ or $\sigma$ used (Weakness 4).
- Section 4.3 (Candidate Generation) clearly separates the coarse and fine branches and explains the rationale for sampling from the predictive distribution rather than taking the mode. The physics-filter paragraph is well-written at the conceptual level but lacks an explicit residual formula (Weakness 4).
- Section 4.4 (Composite Verifier) is the clearest technical section in the paper; the cross-scale decomposition into $Y_{\text{global}}/Y_{\text{local}}$ is explained well and used consistently by the trend, frequency, and spatial terms. Still, none of the three terms is given as an explicit equation (Weakness 4), and the term "multi-scale DTW" from the Introduction's contribution bullet is not obviously matched by anything "multi-scale" in this section's description of $E_{\text{trend}}$ (it describes one DTW alignment between two already-reconciled trend series) — this terminology should be reconciled or clarified.

### Experimental Settings and Results
- The Datasets paragraph is the only place WEATHER-5K is substantively described; it is well-written in isolation but orphaned relative to the rest of Section 5 (Weakness 1).
- The Baselines paragraph's three-group structure (published methods / internal references / alternative search strategies) is clear and matches the structure of the subsequent tables and sections.
- The Metrics paragraph is noticeably terser than the level of detail used elsewhere in the Method section and leaves Moran's $I$, RMSE$_{\mathrm{lw}}$, and ACC undefined (Weakness 5).
- Table `tab:main_results`'s and Table `tab:search_strategies`'s near-duplicate Best-of-$N$ baselines are a recurring source of potential reader confusion (Weakness 8).
- The ablation subsections (Q1)-(Q3), (Q5) are each well-written internally, with a consistent "construct variants → report table → interpret" pattern, but the missing (Q4) and the table/text mismatch in the budget-allocation study (Weaknesses 1 and 6) detract from the otherwise solid structure.
- Figure `fig:scaling` is clearly labeled as an illustrative placeholder, which is appropriate and consistent with the paper's stated placeholder convention.

### Conclusion
- Well-written and appropriately self-critical in isolation (soft physics constraint, single dataset/resolution/variable-set scope, compute/accuracy tradeoff, static clustering).
- Directly contradicts the two-dataset framing established earlier in the paper by stating the evaluation is "confined to a single reanalysis dataset (ERA5)" (Weakness 1) — this needs to be reconciled with Section 3.1/5.1 before submission.
- Uses "ST-TTC" to (re)introduce the method's own name, reinforcing the naming inconsistency noted in Weakness 2.

## Missing References
No critical missing related-work citations were identified beyond the six broken citation keys noted in Weakness 3 (which indicates these six works were *intended* to be cited — the in-text `\citep{}` calls are present — but their BibTeX entries are missing from `references.bib`).

## Minor Issues
- Table `tab:budget_split` and Table `tab:w3_sweep` captions both end with a stray extra period ("...validation RMSE. .}" and "...trend/frequency ratio. .}"), apparently an editing leftover.
- The equation label `eq:selection2` (Section 4.4) suggests an earlier `eq:selection` existed and was removed elsewhere in the document; harmless for compilation but worth cleaning up for internal consistency of labels.
- "ACC" (Section 5.1 Metrics paragraph, Table `tab:aggregate`) is used without being spelled out as "anomaly correlation coefficient" anywhere in the text.
- The phrase "frequency-domain forecasting objectives" (Section 4.4, $E_{\text{FFT}}$ paragraph) cites `\citep{wang2025fredf}`, a key missing from the bibliography (see Weakness 3).
- Minor stylistic note (unrelated to venue standards but worth flagging): no instances of "i.e." were found, and em-dash usage is minimal — the prose style is otherwise clean and consistent.

## Recommendation
**Borderline**

The core idea (test-time Best-of-$N$ search with a physics-aware filter and a multi-scale verifier for frozen spatio-temporal foundation models) is sound and the planned ablation suite is commendably thorough for a thesis-stage paper. However, the manuscript currently contains a significant number of internal-consistency and completeness problems — the orphaned WEATHER-5K dataset contradicted by the Conclusion, the pervasive SC-BoN/ST-TTC naming collision with a cited competing method, six broken citation keys, and missing equations/hyperparameters for the method's core components — that are independent of the acceptable placeholder-number gaps and must be resolved. I would upgrade to Weak Accept once these specific issues are fixed and the promised two-dataset evaluation either materializes in the Results or is consistently scoped down to ERA5 throughout the paper.
