# Reviewer 2 Report

> **Reviewer Focus**: Novelty & Positioning
> **Paper**: Test-Time Computing for Spatio-Temporal Weather Forecasting with Foundation Models
> **Target Venue**: Scopus-indexed applied machine learning / information sciences journal (master's-thesis paper)
> **Review Date**: 2026-11-07

## Summary
The paper proposes Spatial-Cluster Best-of-$N$, a training-free, gradient-free test-time computing (TTC) framework that layers candidate generation, physics-based pre-filtering, and a three-term verifier (trend, frequency, spatial-graph consistency) on top of a frozen time-series foundation model (primarily Chronos-2) to improve zero-shot weather forecasts. The core idea is to generate full-resolution "fine" candidates and cheap cluster-aggregated "coarse" anchors from the same frozen backbone, prune the fine candidates with an advection-PDE residual, and select among survivors by matching them to the coarse anchors.

## Overall Assessment
This is a carefully assembled, application-level contribution that combines several well-established techniques (spectral clustering, TimeMixer-style multiscale downsampling, DTW/FFT trend-seasonal matching, graph-Laplacian smoothness, PDE-residual physics filtering, Best-of-$N$ search) into a coherent pipeline for a genuinely under-explored niche: test-time compute for *frozen, general-purpose* time-series foundation models on *continuous, physically-governed spatial fields*. The Related Work section is unusually thorough for a thesis-derived paper and, to its credit, explicitly and specifically differentiates the proposed approach from the works it most overlaps with (DEFT, SCALER, Chen & Liang's ST-TTC, PINFDiT) rather than relying on vague "different domain" hand-waving. However, the positioning is undermined by several internal inconsistencies that a careful read exposes: the authors' own method is named "ST-TTC" in several sections — the identical acronym the paper itself uses throughout for the directly competing prior work by Chen & Liang (2025) that it spends a full paragraph distinguishing itself from; the Abstract's blanket novelty claim is directly contradicted by the Introduction's own account of that same prior work; and the "two-dataset" evaluation promised in the Experimental Settings is, as written, never delivered in the Results, a fact the Conclusion's own limitations paragraph confirms by contradicting Section 5.1. These are not nitpicks — they strike at the heart of how a Novelty & Positioning reviewer judges whether the claimed gap is real and whether the contribution is properly scoped. The underlying method design is reasonable and the individual novelty claims (frozen/gradient-free regime, cross-scale verification, physics pre-filter for compute efficiency) are defensible for a Scopus-tier applied journal, but the paper needs a consistency pass before its positioning claims can be taken at face value.

## Strengths
1. **Unusually substantive related-work differentiation**: Rather than generic "ours is different" statements, Section 2.3 gives a precise mechanistic contrast with Chen & Liang's ST-TTC ("spectral-domain calibrator with phase-amplitude modulation... updated online via flash gradient descent on a streaming memory queue" vs. the proposed strictly frozen, gradient-free regime), and Section 2.4 gives an equally specific contrast with PINFDiT ("steers a diffusion sampling process rather than performing verification-based selection among candidates from a general-purpose forecaster"). This is exactly the level of specificity a Novelty reviewer wants to see, and I verified independently that the characterizations of Chen & Liang's ST-TTC (NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight, github.com/Onedean/ST-TTC) and PINFDiT (ICLR 2026, Langevin-corrected diffusion transformer) are accurate.
2. **Honest crediting of precedent rather than overclaiming "first"**: Line 40 explicitly states Chen and Liang "recently pioneered test-time computing for spatio-temporal forecasting," and the contributions list (Section 1) never claims to be first at TTC in general, only within the specific frozen/foundation-model/continuum-physics slice of the space. This kind of calibrated claim-making is good practice and increases trust in the rest of the related-work discussion — which makes the Abstract's overclaim (see Weaknesses) all the more avoidable.
3. **A crisp formal device for the central differentiator**: Equation 2 ($\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{0}$) turns the prose distinction between "test-time search" and "test-time adaptation" into something checkable, and it is used consistently afterward (Section 2.3, Section 3.2) to anchor the contrast with Chen & Liang and Wang et al.'s TTA. This is a nice piece of positioning craft.
4. **Dual-domain graph abstraction is a genuine potential differentiator**: Treating grid cells and weather stations uniformly as nodes of a geodesic graph (Section 3.1) is a reasonable unifying move that, if validated, would differentiate the framework from grid-only approaches (GraphCast, ClimODE, ClimaX) that cannot natively handle irregular station networks. The idea itself is sound even though (see Weaknesses) it is not currently backed by any shown result.
5. **Transparent borrowing of the search-strategy evaluation protocol**: Section 5.2's comparison against Random Search / CEM / TuRBO-1 / Surrogate CEM is explicitly attributed to DEFT's protocol ("following the protocol of \citep{le2026deft}," "Baseline suite follows \citep{le2026deft}"), rather than presented as original. This transparency is commendable and should be the model for the rest of the comparison tables.

## Weaknesses

1. **Naming collision with the paper's own key related work** (Major): The authors' method is introduced in the Abstract as "Spatial-Cluster Best-of-$N$ (ST-TTC)" and in the Introduction/Method/Table captions as "SC-BoN" — but from Section 5.3 onward (Q1, Q2, Q3 ablations) and in the Conclusion, the method is referred to and even explicitly re-defined as "ST-TTC" ("We proposed Spatial-Cluster Best-of-$N$ (ST-TTC)..."). This is the *exact same acronym* the paper uses throughout Sections 1, 2.3, and 3.2 for Chen and Liang's prior work, which it is explicitly trying to distinguish itself from on the grounds of not being an "ST-TTC"-style gradient-based calibrator. A reader skimming the Results or Conclusion could easily conclude the authors are claiming or rebranding Chen & Liang's method, which is the opposite of the paper's intended positioning.
   - *Suggestion*: Pick one consistent abbreviation for the proposed method (SC-BoN is the better choice, since it does not collide with any cited prior work) and apply it uniformly across the Abstract, Highlights, every section, every table/figure caption, and the Conclusion. This is a mechanical fix but a critical one before the paper is submitted anywhere.

2. **Abstract's novelty claim is self-contradicted by the Introduction** (Major): The Abstract states "Test-time computing has been explored for scaling reasoning and generation in other domains, but it has not been extended to spatio-temporal forecasting." Thirty lines later, the Introduction states that "Chen and Liang recently pioneered test-time computing for spatio-temporal forecasting, termed ST-TTC." These two statements cannot both be true, and a Novelty & Positioning reviewer will flag the Abstract claim as either inaccurate or, charitably, imprecisely scoped.
   - *Suggestion*: Rewrite the Abstract sentence to carry the same precision the Introduction already has, e.g., "test-time computing has not been extended to spatio-temporal forecasting with *frozen, general-purpose foundation models operating in a gradient-free regime on continuous physical fields*" — the real, defensible gap — rather than the current unqualified claim.

3. **The promised two-dataset evaluation is structurally absent from the Results** (Major): Section 5.1 ("Experimental Settings") describes WEATHER-5K in detail, including its own forecast horizon configuration ($L_H=48$, $L_F=24$) and variable set (temperature, dewpoint, wind direction, wind speed, sea-level pressure), framing it as one of "two complementary weather datasets" the paper evaluates on. Yet every single table and figure in Sections 5.2 (Main Results), 5.3 (Search Strategies), 5.4 (Ablations Q1/Q2/Q3/Q5), and 5.5 (Analysis) is ERA5-only — there is no WEATHER-5K table, not even a placeholder one. This is confirmed directly by the paper's own Conclusion, which lists as a caveat: "our evaluation is confined to a single reanalysis dataset (ERA5)... so whether the reported gains transfer to other datasets... remains to be verified." This is not a "pending final numbers" situation (which I am instructed not to penalize) — it is a missing *section/table structure* for an entire dataset the paper claims, twice, to evaluate on. Consequently, the generality claim underlying Section 3.1's "the framework treats $\mathcal{V}$ uniformly... regardless of their origin" is currently entirely unsupported, even in placeholder form.
   - *Suggestion*: Either (a) add WEATHER-5K results tables/subsections (even as placeholders, consistent with the rest of the paper's convention) to Sections 5.2–5.4, and update the Conclusion's limitations paragraph to no longer claim single-dataset confinement, or (b) if WEATHER-5K is genuinely out of scope for this submission, remove or clearly re-scope the Section 5.1 paragraph (and the dual-domain framing in Section 3.1) to avoid promising an evaluation that will not appear.

4. **No ensemble-averaging (Mean-of-N) baseline** (Moderate): The paper's reference points are single-pass (N=1) and a "simple-scorer Best-of-$N$" that still *selects* one candidate. There is no baseline that simply averages/aggregates the $N$ already-drawn candidates (e.g., per-point mean or median), which is the cheapest and most natural alternative use of the same sampling budget, and which the Best-of-$N$-theory literature the paper itself cites (Huang et al. 2025) implicitly motivates as a comparison point, since selection and aggregation trade off bias and variance differently.
   - *Suggestion*: Add a Mean-of-$N$ (or Median-of-$N$) row to Table 2/3, since this is the natural "does selection actually beat trivial aggregation at this budget" check that strengthens the case for the proposed verifier-based selection mechanism.

5. **Claimed study of Best-of-$N$ "over-optimization" is not actually delivered** (Moderate): Section 2.1 states that the risk described by Huang et al. (2025) — that increasing $N$ with an imperfect verifier can cause *optimization degradation* — "is a risk we explicitly study" in the Analysis/compute-budget section. But Section 5.3.2's experiment and its schematic Figure 2 show only monotonic improvement with diminishing returns (saturation near $N\approx16$), never actual degradation at large $N$. Saturation and degradation are different phenomena, and the paper currently only demonstrates the former while claiming to study the latter.
   - *Suggestion*: Either extend the $N$ sweep far enough (or choose a setting) where degradation is empirically visible, or soften the Section 2.1 claim to "a risk we discuss" rather than "explicitly study," and reserve the stronger claim for if/when degradation is actually shown.

6. **Research Gap #1 is phrased more broadly than the paper's own Related Work supports** (Minor-moderate): Section 1 claims "there is no training-free mechanism to assess and enforce physical plausibility at test time." Section 2.4 then describes PINFDiT as doing exactly this (injecting a physics energy term at inference into a pretrained, frozen diffusion transformer via Langevin dynamics, with zero retraining). The eventual differentiation given (selection-among-candidates vs. steering-the-sampler) is valid and specific, but the initial gap statement overclaims relative to it.
   - *Suggestion*: Narrow Gap #1's phrasing up front to match the more precise distinction given later, e.g., "no training-free mechanism that treats physical plausibility as a pre-filter over independently drawn candidates from a general-purpose multivariate forecaster, as opposed to physics-guided steering of a single generative sampling trajectory."

7. **Several in-text citations have no corresponding bibliography entry** (Minor, but relevant to verifying positioning claims): `\citep{han2024weather5k}` (line 209), `\citep{wang2025fredf}` (line 193), `\citep{shuman2013gsp}` (line 195), `\citep{scheuerer2015variogram}` (line 195), `\citep{deboer2005cem}` (lines 273, 289), and `\citep{eriksson2019turbo}` (lines 273, 290) all appear in `manuscript.md` but have no matching key in `paper/references.bib`. I independently confirmed WEATHER-5K is a real, published dataset (Han et al., arXiv:2406.14399 / the WEATHER-5K GitHub benchmark), so this looks like an omission rather than fabrication, but as submitted the bibliography would not resolve and reviewers/readers cannot check the remaining five.
   - *Suggestion*: Reconcile `references.bib` with every `\citep` key in the manuscript before the next revision; a missing-bib-entry sweep is a five-minute fix that otherwise looks sloppy to a venue editor.

8. **Physics pre-filter's portability to the second dataset is unaddressed** (Minor-moderate): The advection/continuity PDE residual (Section 4.2) is formulated around temperature transported by horizontal wind components (u, v) — exactly ERA5's t2m/u10/v10. WEATHER-5K instead provides wind *direction and speed* (polar form) plus dewpoint and sea-level pressure, none of which the described residual obviously covers. If WEATHER-5K is to be evaluated (see Weakness 3), the paper needs to state how the physics filter is adapted; as written, the "treats $\mathcal{V}$ uniformly" generality claim applies to the *spatial* graph construction but has not been shown to extend to the *physics* component of the pipeline.
   - *Suggestion*: Add a short paragraph (even in the Datasets or Method section) specifying how u/v wind components are derived from direction/speed for WEATHER-5K, and whether/how the filter is modified (or disabled) for variables it does not model (dewpoint, pressure).

## Questions for Authors
1. Given the collision with Chen & Liang's own "ST-TTC," do you intend to standardize on "SC-BoN" (or a different name) throughout the final manuscript, including the Results and Conclusion sections where "ST-TTC" currently reappears as the primary name?
2. Will WEATHER-5K results actually be included in the Main Results/Ablation tables of the final version, or is Section 5.1's WEATHER-5K description intended for a future paper? If included, how is the physics pre-filter adapted to WEATHER-5K's variable parameterization (wind direction/speed rather than u/v; dewpoint and sea-level pressure, which the advection residual does not cover)?
3. Why does the baseline suite omit a simple Mean-of-$N$/Median-of-$N$ aggregation baseline, and would the authors be willing to add it given it is the cheapest alternative use of the same $N$-sample budget?
4. Can the Abstract's claim that TTC "has not been extended to spatio-temporal forecasting" be reconciled with, or rewritten around, the Introduction's acknowledgment that Chen & Liang already did exactly this?
5. Beyond the components individually cited (spectral clustering, TimeMixer downsampling, DTW/FFT matching, graph-Laplacian smoothness, PDE-residual filtering), what is the authors' own view of the single most original algorithmic idea in the paper, as distinct from the combination/engineering novelty? This would help calibrate the significance claim for a Scopus-tier venue.

## Detailed Comments

### Abstract
- Strong, specific framing of the problem (no mechanism to spend extra test-time compute, no way to enforce physical/spatial structure). The overclaim flagged in Weakness 2 is the main issue; otherwise concise and readable.
- The Abstract's own name for the method ("ST-TTC") is inconsistent with the rest of the paper's "SC-BoN" (see Weakness 1).

### Introduction
- The three "research gaps" (physical plausibility, multi-scale spatial coupling, inference-budget efficiency) are clearly enumerated and map reasonably well onto the four contributions, which is good structural discipline.
- Gap #1's phrasing is broader than what Section 2.4 later supports (Weakness 6).
- The contributions list (Section 1) scopes the evaluation to ERA5 only ("multi-variable ERA5 reanalysis data... systematically investigating compute budget scaling, verifier component sensitivity, spatial clustering topologies, and cross-backbone transferability") — it never mentions WEATHER-5K, which is only introduced later in Section 5.1. This reinforces that the two-dataset framing was added to the Experimental Settings without being threaded back through the Introduction, Results, or Conclusion.

### Related Work
- Organizationally this is the strongest section of the paper for a Novelty & Positioning read: five well-scoped subsections, each ending with an explicit contrast statement rather than a pure literature list.
- Section 2.3's distinction between "test-time search and editing" (DEFT, SCALER) and "test-time adaptation and online calibration" (Wang et al., Chen & Liang) is a useful taxonomy that sharpens where the proposed method sits.
- Missing: classical ensemble/multi-model NWP post-processing literature (generate multiple perturbed forecasts, verify/weight/select), which is the closest *within-domain* precedent for "generate, score, select" and would strengthen rather than weaken the positioning if acknowledged and distinguished (e.g., on the grounds that NWP ensembles perturb initial conditions/physics parameterizations of a numerical model rather than sampling a frozen learned predictive distribution). Also missing: the original WeatherBench benchmark paper (Rasp et al.), cited by name in the Metrics paragraph of Section 5.1 but not in the bibliography; the self-consistency/majority-voting literature (e.g., Wang et al. 2022) that the "simple-scorer... consensus-based" baseline (Section 5.2) conceptually descends from.

### Problem Definition
- The unification of grid cells and stations as nodes of one geodesic graph (Section 3.1) is conceptually clean and well-motivated (handles pole convergence, irregular spacing).
- Equation 2's frozen/gradient-free constraint is a nice formal anchor for the positioning argument, used consistently later. Good.

### Method
- The four-stage "generate, score, select" pipeline (Section 4.1) is clearly laid out with a helpful figure and summary table.
- The cross-scale decomposition into $Y_{\text{global}}$/$Y_{\text{local}}$ (Section 4.4) and the specific choice to apply trend/frequency terms at the coarse scale and the spatial term at the residual scale is a sensible, specific design choice rather than an arbitrary combination — this is the most genuinely novel technical piece of the paper, more so than any individual component.
- The physics pre-filter (Section 4.2) is honestly caveated as a "soft filter" rather than a hard constraint, which is appropriately modest.

### Experimental Settings and Results
- The dataset description (Section 5.1) is detailed and well-specified for ERA5, but introduces the WEATHER-5K/dataset-scope inconsistency discussed in Weakness 3.
- The baseline taxonomy (published methods / internal references / alternative search optimizers) in Section 5.2 is sensible and the "Train?" column is a nice touch for a training-free positioning argument, correctly flagging PINFDiT as the only other no-training baseline.
- Weakness 4 (missing Mean-of-$N$) and Weakness 5 (over-optimization claim not delivered) apply most directly here.

### Ablation Study / Analysis
- The five ablation axes (verifier components, compute budget, backbone generality, clustering strategy, budget allocation/weight sensitivity) are a genuinely thorough sensitivity-analysis plan for a thesis-level paper and, once filled in with real numbers, would substantially strengthen the significance argument.
- The clustering ablation's "physiographic (multi-modal)" variant is explicitly flagged as exploratory and not part of the main pipeline (Section 5.4.4) — appropriately honest, though it does raise the question of why an exploratory variant warrants a full ablation table row rather than a shorter mention.

### Conclusion
- Admirably candid about limitations (soft physics constraint, single-dataset/resolution/variable-set scope, compute-accuracy tradeoff, static clustering). The "single reanalysis dataset (ERA5)" admission here is the most direct evidence of the dataset-scope inconsistency flagged in Weakness 3 and should be reconciled with Section 5.1 before submission.

## Missing References
- Rasp et al., "WeatherBench: A benchmark dataset for data-driven weather forecasting" — cited by name ("following WeatherBench and GraphCast") in Section 5.1 but absent from the bibliography.
- Wang et al. 2022, "Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models" — the conceptual ancestor of the "consensus-based" simple-scorer baseline used in Section 5.2; worth citing to properly attribute that baseline's lineage.
- General ensemble NWP / multi-model ensemble post-processing literature (e.g., classical ensemble Kalman filter or Bayesian model averaging work in operational NWP) — the closest within-domain precedent to "generate multiple candidates and combine/select," currently entirely absent from Section 2.5's otherwise careful graph-based/ML-centric framing.

## Minor Issues
- Inconsistent method name ("ST-TTC" vs. "SC-BoN") across Abstract/Highlights/Introduction/Method vs. Results/Conclusion — see Weakness 1.
- Six undefined `\citep` keys relative to the provided `paper/references.bib` (Weakness 7).
- Table~\ref{tab:ablation_verifier}'s caption and Table~\ref{tab:budget_split}/\ref{tab:w3_sweep}'s captions have a stray trailing " ." (double space before a period) — minor LaTeX typo to clean up.
- Section 5.4.2 numbers $N$ up to 64 and discusses "the larger extremes of the sweep are dominated on cost without a commensurate accuracy benefit," but the schematic Figure 2's $x$-axis only goes to 64 with a flat tail — once real data replaces the placeholder, make sure the figure and the "diminishing returns beyond $N\approx16$" prose agree exactly on the location of the knee.
- The phrase "Living at a coarse spatial (cluster) and temporal (downsampled) scale" (Section 4.2) is a slightly awkward sentence fragment; consider rephrasing for flow.

## Confidence
4/5. I read the manuscript in full and independently verified the key related-work characterizations (Chen & Liang's ST-TTC, PINFDiT, SCALER, DEFT's baseline protocol) against public sources. My confidence is not higher only because I could not verify the unlisted citations (`wang2025fredf`, `shuman2013gsp`, `scheuerer2015variogram`, `deboer2005cem`, `eriksson2019turbo`) and because final numerical results are placeholders, as instructed.

## Recommendation
**Borderline**

The underlying method and the related-work scholarship are solid enough for a Scopus-tier applied ML/information-sciences venue, and I am explicitly not penalizing the placeholder numbers per the review instructions. However, three structural positioning problems — the "ST-TTC" naming collision with the very prior work the paper differentiates itself from, the Abstract's novelty claim being directly contradicted by the Introduction, and the complete absence of the promised WEATHER-5K results/validation of the paper's "domain-agnostic" claim — mean the novelty and significance of the contribution cannot currently be fully assessed on the paper's own terms. Resolving these three issues, adding a Mean-of-$N$ baseline, and reconciling the bibliography would move this toward a clear Accept once real results are in place.
