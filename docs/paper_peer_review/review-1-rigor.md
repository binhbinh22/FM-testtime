# Review: Spatial-Cluster Best-of-N (ST-TTC) for Spatio-Temporal Weather Forecasting

**Reviewer lens:** Methodological rigor
**Venue assumed:** Reputable applied-ML / time-series forecasting venue (journal-style submission, elsarticle format)
**Manuscript status acknowledged:** Early draft; numeric results are placeholders ("--", "[X]"). This review does not penalize missing numbers and focuses on whether the method, evaluation design, and the claims that surround the (future) numbers are sound as currently specified.

## Summary

The paper proposes Spatial-Cluster Best-of-N (ST-TTC), a training-free test-time-computing wrapper around a frozen multivariate time-series foundation model (Chronos-2) for gridded weather forecasting. The method (1) clusters the spatial grid once via spectral clustering of a great-circle kNN graph, (2) draws coarse (cluster-aggregated, temporally downsampled) and fine (full-resolution) candidate forecasts from the same frozen backbone, (3) prunes the fine candidates with a soft physics-plausibility residual, and (4) selects the final forecast by minimizing a three-term verifier (DTW trend, FFT periodicity, graph-Laplacian spatial consistency) scored against the best-matching coarse anchor. The experimental plan includes a main comparison table, four labeled ablations (verifier components, compute budget, backbone generality, integration topology, clustering strategy), and two further analyses (budget split, weight sensitivity), all currently unpopulated.

## Strengths

1. **Clear, falsifiable problem formulation.** Section 2 gives a precise notation for the frozen backbone, the i.i.d. candidate draws, and the argmin selection rule (Eqs. 1–2), and explicitly separates test-time computing from test-time adaptation (Section 3.3 of Related Work) with a correct, defensible criterion (whether $\theta$ is ever updated). This kind of explicit scoping is exactly what a rigor-focused reviewer wants to see up front.

2. **Honest treatment of cross-paper comparisons.** The main-results text and table caption explicitly flag that PINT/ClimODE/ClimaX/PINFDiT numbers are copied from their original papers under possibly different setups and should be read as "a reference rather than a strictly controlled comparison" (Section 4.2). The internal author TODO comments in the source even pre-empt the reviewer's concern ("KHÔNG tuyên bố SOTA tuyệt đối"). This is good scientific hygiene and should be preserved once real numbers are filled in.

3. **A genuinely comprehensive ablation menu.** Five distinct ablation axes (verifier leave-one-out, compute-budget scaling, backbone generality, integration topology, clustering strategy) plus two sensitivity analyses (branch split, verifier weights) is an unusually thorough plan for isolating where the method's benefit comes from. If executed as described, it would let a reader distinguish "the verifier helps," "the physics filter helps," "the result is backbone-specific," and "the result is an artifact of extra compute" (via the random-selection control in Q1), which are exactly the confounds a skeptical reviewer would otherwise raise.

4. **Correct high-level handling of the physics term as approximate, not exact.** The paper states plainly that 2m temperature is not an exactly conserved quantity and that the residual therefore "acts only as a soft filter" (Section 3.4) rather than a hard physical constraint, and repeats this caveat in the Conclusion's limitations paragraph. Getting this framing right (rather than overselling a "physics-constrained" forecast) is a meaningful sign of care, even if the mechanism's description has an internal inconsistency noted below.

## Weaknesses

1. **The Conclusion is written as if the experiment already succeeded (Major).** Section 6's first paragraph makes fully declarative, unhedged claims: "ST-TTC achieved best or comparable per-variable MAE and RMSE among training-free methods, remained competitive with trained baselines, and delivered the most consistent improvement in spatial coherence." Unlike Section 4.2 and the ablation prose, which at least mark unknown magnitudes with "[X]%", this paragraph contains no placeholders at all, and reads as a pre-committed success narrative rather than a summary of actual findings. The caption of Table 8 has the same issue ("A consistent positive Δ across backbones indicates..."). This is the single biggest over-claiming risk in the manuscript: if real results diverge from this narrative (e.g., the spatial term does not help, or one backbone regresses), there is a strong temptation to either suppress that result or leave outdated text uncorrected.
   - *Suggestion*: Rewrite the Conclusion's first paragraph and Table 8's caption with the same bracketed-placeholder convention used elsewhere (e.g., "ST-TTC achieved [best/comparable/worse — TBD]..."), or defer all outcome language to a results-contingent revision pass that is explicitly checklisted before submission.

2. **Several formulas load-bearing for reproducibility are never written down.** The physics residual (the actual advection/continuity finite-difference expression), the "activity penalty," and how the two are combined into the single score used for the top-$K_A$ cut (Section 3.4) are described only in prose. Similarly, $E_{\text{FFT}}$'s $\ell_1$ distance does not specify the frequency truncation/bin count or whether phase is used, and the graph-Laplacian roughness functional for $E_{\text{spatial}}$ is never given as an equation. The spectral-clustering graph's $k$ (neighbors) and $\sigma$ (bandwidth) are described only qualitatively, with no value or even a "[TBD]" placeholder (unlike the backbone checkpoint and context length, which the authors do mark as "[TBD]").
   - *Suggestion*: Add explicit equations (even in an appendix) for the physics residual, the activity penalty, their combination rule, the FFT distance (with bin range and phase/magnitude choice stated), and the Laplacian smoothness functional. Give numeric or "[TBD]" values for $k$, $\sigma$, and the final tuned $(w_1,w_2,w_3)$, none of which currently appear anywhere in the paper, even as placeholders.

3. **The "simple-scorer Best-of-N" baseline is not operationally defined.** Section 4.2 describes it only as using "a plain error-based scorer" computing "MAE" (Section 4.1, Baselines), but the problem definition explicitly forbids access to the ground-truth future at selection time (Section 2). It is therefore unclear what reference the simple scorer's MAE is computed against. If it uses a different reference than the proposed verifier (e.g., persistence, candidate-ensemble mean, or something else), the comparison is confounded by more than just "simple vs. composite" scoring, and a reader cannot tell whether the entire baseline is even well-posed under the paper's own stated setting.
   - *Suggestion*: State explicitly what the simple scorer measures each candidate against (e.g., deviation from the ensemble mean of the $N$ draws, or from a persistence/climatology baseline), and confirm it respects the same no-ground-truth constraint as the proposed verifier.

4. **"# backbone calls" is used as the compute metric across methods with structurally different per-call cost.** The main table and several ablations report compute as a call count ($1$, $N$, etc.), but a coarse-branch call (forecasting $M{=}32$ cluster-mean series, temporally downsampled) and a fine-branch call (forecasting $|\mathcal{V}|{=}2048$ full-resolution series) are not remotely comparable in FLOPs or wall-clock time. Treating them as equal units means "Ours" at $N{=}20$ (15 fine + 5 cheap coarse) is not actually iso-compute with a hypothetical competitor spending all $N{=}20$ calls on full-resolution forecasts, even though the table would report both as "$N$." Latency is reported separately in some ablations (Table 6, Table 9) but not folded into the main results table at all.
   - *Suggestion*: Either report wall-clock latency or FLOPs alongside call count in the main results table (Table 4), or explicitly state the per-call cost ratio between coarse and fine calls so a reader can convert "# calls" into comparable compute units.

5. **Q4 (integration topology) ablation table omits the method actually being argued for, and overloads the word "late."** The text states that "verification-only selection, the design used in our main pipeline, achieves the best balance between accuracy and cost among the three topologies in Table 9" — but Table 9 has no row labeled "verification-only" or "Ours"; its rows are "No Centroid," "Early Conditioning" (two variants), and "Late Anchoring" (two variants). A reader cannot locate the claimed winner in the table that is supposed to support the claim. Compounding this, the main method is introduced in prose as a "late, post-hoc use of the anchor" (opening sentence of Section 5.3), while a different compared alternative is separately named "Late Anchoring" with a distinct mechanism (linear blending of outputs). Using "late" for two different things in the same subsection is likely to confuse readers trying to map the ablation back onto the main method.
   - *Suggestion*: Add an explicit "Verification-only (Ours)" row to Table 9 with its own numbers, and rename one of the two overlapping concepts, e.g., call the main method's design "anchor-for-scoring" or "post-hoc verification" and reserve "Late Anchoring" exclusively for the output-blending alternative.

6. **Inconsistent description of the physics filter's mechanism between Methods and Conclusion.** Section 3.4 implements the filter as a hard, discrete top-$K_A$ truncation: "retain the $K_A$ candidates with the lowest resulting score," discarding the other $N_A-K_A$ candidates entirely before the verifier ever sees them. The Conclusion's limitations paragraph instead describes the same mechanism as the residual being only able to "down-weight physically implausible candidates," which describes a continuous, non-discarding weighting scheme, not a hard top-$K$ cut. These are two different operations (discard vs. reweight), and the paper should use the one it actually implements consistently. This matters because a reader trying to judge whether "soft constraint" claims are accurate needs to know whether implausible candidates are merely penalized (and could still be selected if nothing else is better) or are removed from consideration altogether (as Section 3.4 actually does).
   - *Suggestion*: Use "hard pre-filter based on a soft (approximate) physical residual" consistently, and correct the Conclusion's "down-weight" wording to match the top-$K_A$ truncation actually described in Section 3.4.

7. **No statistical uncertainty is reported or planned anywhere in the design.** Candidate generation is explicitly stochastic (Eq. 1, i.i.d. draws), and the cited \citep{huang2025bestofn} result that naive Best-of-$N$ scaling can degrade under an imperfect verifier is invoked in the Related Work as a risk the paper claims to "return to" (Section 3.1) — yet none of the result tables (main results, Q1–Q5, budget/weight analyses) include variance, confidence intervals, number of test windows, or a plan for repeated sampling seeds. Given the explicit acknowledgment that Best-of-$N$ can be noisy, point estimates alone will not let a reader judge whether an "[X]% RMSE improvement" is a robust effect or sampling noise.
   - *Suggestion*: Report mean ± standard deviation (or bootstrap CI) over multiple random seeds of the $N$-candidate draw, and/or over the full set of test windows, and state the number of test windows (sliding-window count, stride) used to compute each averaged metric. A simple paired test (e.g., Wilcoxon signed-rank across test windows) for the headline RMSE comparison against the single-pass floor would substantially strengthen the soundness of the main claim.

8. **The "Train?" binary column conflates two different notions of "no training."** Table 4 marks only PINFDiT and "Ours" as "No" under Train?, but the text describes PINFDiT as injecting a physics-residual term "into a pre-trained diffusion transformer" (Section 3.4 of Related Work). If that diffusion transformer was itself trained/fine-tuned on weather or climate data (as is typical for diffusion-based weather models), then PINFDiT and Chronos-2 are not on equal footing with respect to domain exposure: one is a domain-specific model that is not further trained at inference time, while the other is a fully general-purpose, zero-shot foundation model never exposed to ERA5-like data at all. Collapsing this distinction into one Yes/No column risks implying the two "No-training" entries are more comparable than they are.
   - *Suggestion*: Add a footnote or an extra column distinguishing "no inference-time training" from "zero-shot with respect to the target domain/dataset," and state explicitly whether PINFDiT's backbone was pretrained on weather data.

9. **Internal numeric contradiction around backbone context length.** Table 3 lists Chronos-2's context length as 512, identical to the original Chronos. Yet the adjoining prose argues Chronos-2 was chosen partly because it "offers a comparatively long context window" (Section 4.1, Backbone selection), and the Conclusion states that one of the three evaluated history/horizon settings uses a history of 1024 ("512/64, 512/128, 1024/96"), which would already exceed the context length the paper's own table assigns to the backbone. These three statements cannot all be correct simultaneously.
   - *Suggestion*: Reconcile Table 3's context-length value with the actual value used in experiments (which the Backbone paragraph itself marks as "[TBD]"), and verify that 1024 is a supported context length before including the 1024/96 setting, or remove that setting if it is not.

10. **Backbone-generality ablation (Q3) names more/different models than the surrounding prose promises.** Section 4.1 (Backbone paragraph) and Section 5.4's opening sentence both say the generality study uses three additional backbones: Chronos-1, TimesFM, Moirai. Table 8, however, lists five rows: Chronos-1, Chronos-2, TimesFM, **TimesFM-3**, **Moirai-2** — introducing two specific versioned models never mentioned anywhere else in the paper (including Table 3's own comparison, which lists plain "TimesFM" and "Moirai"). The authors' own source-level TODO flags this as unresolved ("xác nhận lại tên/phiên bản chính xác"), but as it stands the table does not match the method text that introduces it.
   - *Suggestion*: Fix the backbone roster so Table 3, the Backbone paragraph, Section 5.4's prose, and Table 8 all name the same, version-exact set of models, and confirm each supports whatever sampling interface the fine/coarse branches require before running the ablation.

11. **The min-over-anchors matching rule is not justified against simpler alternatives.** Eq. 10 scores each fine candidate against whichever of the $N_C$ coarse anchors gives it the lowest (most favorable) score. This is the most permissive possible matching rule: a candidate only needs to resemble one out of $N_C$ noisy coarse samples, not their consensus. Since the coarse anchors are themselves noisy draws from the same imperfect backbone (just at a coarser scale), this could let outlier anchors validate otherwise-poor fine candidates, diluting exactly the discriminative power the trend/FFT terms are meant to provide.
   - *Suggestion*: Either justify the min-over-anchors choice explicitly (e.g., empirically compare it against scoring against the ensemble mean/median of the $N_C$ anchors), or report this as an additional ablation axis alongside Q1–Q5.

12. **Minor reproducibility/terminology gaps.**
    - The DTW choice for $E_{\text{trend}}$ is not motivated given that the moving-average windows are explicitly matched across scales "so that both extract the trend over the same physical time span" (Section 3.5), which already removes the temporal misalignment DTW is designed to tolerate. A simpler L1/L2 distance might be equally valid and more interpretable; the paper should justify the extra warping flexibility or drop it.
    - The i.i.d.-sampling language in Eq. 1 and Section 3.4 ("we draw these candidates from the backbone's predictive distribution") should be checked against how Chronos-2 and the other three backbones (which differ in tokenization and output representation, per Section 5.4) actually generate multiple outputs; if any backbone exposes multiple forecasts via fixed quantile levels rather than independent stochastic draws, "i.i.d." is not the correct characterization and the Best-of-$N$ diversity argument in the Introduction should be adjusted accordingly.
    - No ablation varies the number of spatial clusters $M$ itself (only the clustering *method* at fixed $M{=}32$ is varied in Q5); a brief sensitivity sweep over $M$ would strengthen the default-configuration justification.
    - "Following WeatherBench and GraphCast convention" (Section 4.1, Metrics) cites only \citep{lam2023graphcast}; the original WeatherBench paper (Rasp et al.) should also be cited if the normalized-MSE convention is attributed to it by name.
    - No code/data release statement, hardware spec, software versions, or random-seed policy is given; for a method whose core claim rests on stochastic sampling, this is worth adding before submission.

## Questions for Authors

1. What exactly does the "simple-scorer Best-of-$N$" baseline's MAE term measure against, given that ground truth is unavailable at selection time by the paper's own problem definition?
2. Is Chronos-2's multi-candidate output actually i.i.d. sampling from a predictive distribution, or a fixed set of quantile levels (deterministic given the input)? Does this differ across the four backbones used in the generality study (Chronos-1, Chronos-2, TimesFM, Moirai), and if so, how does that affect the validity of pooling them into one "backbone-agnostic" claim?
3. How many test windows (start times, stride) make up the 2017–2018 test period evaluation, and is any form of repeated-seed or cross-window variance reported for the headline RMSE/Moran's I improvements?
4. What are the final tuned values of $w_1, w_2, w_3$, and of the physics filter's internal combination between the PDE residual and the activity penalty? Is there a numeric (not just qualitative) guard against degenerate flat candidates?
5. Was PINFDiT's diffusion-transformer backbone itself pretrained on weather/climate data, and if so, should the "Train?" column in Table 4 distinguish this from Chronos-2's fully domain-agnostic zero-shot pretraining?

## Recommendation

**Major Revision.**

The core idea is sound and the experimental plan is unusually thorough for a draft at this stage; none of the issues above require abandoning the method. However, before numeric results are added, the authors should (a) rewrite the Conclusion to match the hedged, placeholder-aware style used in Section 4–5, (b) add the missing formulas and hyperparameter values needed for reproducibility, (c) fix the Q4 table to include the actual method as a labeled row and de-overload the "late" terminology, (d) define the simple-scorer baseline precisely, and (e) commit to reporting variance/significance given the explicitly acknowledged stochasticity of Best-of-$N$. Addressing these would move this from a promising but under-specified draft to a submission whose eventual numbers could actually be trusted to mean what the text says they mean.
