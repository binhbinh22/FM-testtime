# Review — Novelty & Positioning

**Paper**: "Test-Time Computing for Spatio-Temporal Weather Forecasting with Foundation Models" (Spatial-Cluster Best-of-$N$ / ST-TTC)
**Target venue**: `\journal{TBD}` (elsarticle preprint; applied-ML / time-series forecasting venue)
**Reviewer lens**: Novelty & Positioning (secondary attention to soundness, clarity, significance, reproducibility)
**Manuscript status**: Early draft with placeholder results ("--", "[X]"). This review does **not** penalize missing numbers; it evaluates the contribution claim, the related-work delta, and the honesty of the positioning.

## Summary

The paper proposes ST-TTC, a training-free test-time-computing (TTC) wrapper for a frozen multivariate time-series foundation model (Chronos-2) applied to gridded weather forecasting. The method (1) spectrally clusters the spatial grid once into $M$ regions, (2) draws coarse, cluster-aggregated "anchor" forecasts and full-resolution "fine" candidate forecasts from the same frozen backbone, (3) prunes fine candidates with a finite-difference advection/continuity physics residual, and (4) selects the final forecast via a linear combination of trend (DTW), frequency (FFT), and graph-Laplacian spatial-consistency terms scored against the best-matching coarse anchor. The central claim is that this is the first extension of generate-score-select TTC to a *spatially coupled* forecasting setting, with the main empirical differentiator expected to be spatial coherence (Moran's $I$) rather than raw MAE/RMSE.

## Strengths

1. **Explicit, falsifiable delta statement against the three closest works.** Unlike many related-work sections that merely list citations, Section 2.3/2.4 makes a specific claim: ST-TTC extends DEFT/SCALER "along two dimensions... a spatial dimension... and an explicit physical-plausibility term," and explicitly separates its physics residual's *role* (pre-filter) from PINFDiT's *role* (Langevin steering term) despite using a similar residual. This is the right way to argue novelty — specific and checkable — even though (see Weaknesses) some of the specific claims need correction.

2. **Clean TTC-vs-TTA scoping (Section 2.3).** Explicitly distinguishing test-time *computing* (frozen $\theta$) from test-time *adaptation* (gradient updates to $\theta$), and stating why TTA baselines are therefore out of scope, is a precise and defensible positioning choice that preempts an obvious reviewer objection.

3. **Unusually honest hedging for a methods paper.** The "Train?" column in Table main_results, the explicit "numbers are as-reported... may use a different experimental setup... reference rather than strictly controlled comparison" caveat, and the Conclusion's own limitations paragraph (soft physics filter, single dataset/resolution, static clustering) are exactly the kind of self-aware framing that a novelty-focused reviewer wants to see, and it meaningfully increases trust in the rest of the related-work claims.

4. **The ablation design is built to interrogate the novelty claims, not just tune hyperparameters.** Q1–Q5 (verifier components, compute scaling, backbone generality, integration topology, clustering strategy) are each aimed at a specific "is this really where the gain comes from" question — e.g., Q3 directly tests whether the gain is Chronos-2-specific, Q4 directly tests whether the paper's "verification-only, late" design choice (its core architectural claim) is actually better than earlier/tighter coupling. This is a stronger-than-typical ablation plan for substantiating a positioning argument once real numbers are filled in.

5. **No new backbone is trained; the coarse branch reuses the same frozen model.** This is a genuine (if understated — see Weakness 3) point of distinction from SCALER, whose coarse-shape model is a separately trained lightweight transformer. The paper does not currently make this contrast explicit, but the underlying design fact is real and favorable to the novelty claim.

## Weaknesses

1. **(Major) DEFT's "verifier" is mischaracterized as a free, autonomous scoring function.** The manuscript states that DEFT "introduces a verifier that edits its forecast at inference time" (Sec. 2.3) and "uses a separate verifier to edit its forecast" (Intro). I checked the DEFT paper (arXiv 2607.19659, "Expert-Guided Forecast Editing for Time-Series Foundation Models"): its scoring signal is explicitly "an expensive expert evaluator" consulted under "a tight query budget" — i.e., DEFT's setting assumes access to a costly external oracle (human expert or expensive simulator) and frames the problem as exploit–explore budget allocation over expert queries. This is categorically different from ST-TTC's composite verifier, which is computed for free from the history/physics/graph with no external oracle and no query budget at all. As written, the text blurs this distinction, which (a) is factually imprecise, and (b) actually *undersells* ST-TTC's novelty — the real differentiator ("we need no costly oracle; DEFT does") is stronger than the one currently argued ("we add a spatial dimension").
   - *Suggestion*: Rewrite the DEFT comparison to state explicitly that DEFT operates under a budgeted, externally-scored (expert-in-the-loop) feedback model, whereas ST-TTC's verifier is a fully self-contained, zero-additional-cost function of the history and candidates themselves. Use this contrast, not just the spatial/physics one, as a primary novelty argument.

2. **(Major) PINFDiT's scope claim ("single (non-spatial) series") needs re-verification and is likely inaccurate as stated.** Section 2.4 asserts PINFDiT "operates on a single (non-spatial) series and does not model interactions between grid points." Public descriptions of PINFDiT (ICLR 2026, Cao et al., "Energy-Based Physics-Informed Diffusion Transformers for General-Purpose Time Series Tasks") describe its evaluation as covering "multivariate forecasting with imperfect data," which directly contradicts a blanket "single series" characterization. It is plausible that PINFDiT treats channels independently even in its multivariate experiments (i.e., no explicit spatial *graph*/adjacency structure), in which case the paper's underlying novelty point would survive, but the current wording is not the right way to say it and risks being flagged as a factual error by any reviewer who knows PINFDiT's experiments.
   - *Suggestion*: Re-read PINFDiT's multivariate experiments section before the next draft and replace the blanket claim with the precise one actually supported (e.g., "PINFDiT's physics energy term is computed per channel/series, without an explicit spatial adjacency structure linking co-located variables," if that is what is true), citing the specific experiment/table.

3. **(Major) The structural kinship with SCALER's coarse-anchors-fine design is understated, and the authors' own in-source TODO comment admits the comparison has not yet been verified carefully.** The source contains an untranslated draft note: "*nếu có thời gian, nên đọc kỹ SCALER... trước khi nộp*" ("if time permits, should read SCALER carefully before submission") — i.e., the authors themselves flag that this very recent, closely related concurrent paper has not been fully verified. Independently, SCALER's core mechanism (a lightweight model first produces a coarse "shape," which then anchors/guides iterative fine-grained refinement by an LLM) is structurally the same *generate-coarse-anchor-then-resolve-fine* pattern underlying ST-TTC's two-branch design — just instantiated along the temporal/token axis rather than the spatial/cluster axis. The current text differentiates only on "single series vs. spatial," which is true but does not engage with the possibility that the coarse-anchor-guides-fine *idea itself* is the shared ancestor, with spatial-ness being an application-level variation.
   - *Suggestion*: Before submission, read SCALER in full (per the authors' own note) and add an explicit contrast on the *mechanism*, not just the *domain*: SCALER trains a separate coarse model and uses the coarse signal to actively condition/guide generation (closer to the paper's own "early conditioning" ablation variant, which Table ablation_topology shows is the most accurate but most costly option); ST-TTC reuses the identical frozen backbone for the coarse branch and uses the coarse signal only for post-hoc verification (the "late, verification-only" variant). This is a sharper and more defensible distinction than "single series vs. spatial" alone.

4. **(Major) No engagement with ensemble/stochastic weather forecasting post-processing, which is arguably a closer intellectual ancestor than NLP/video TTC.** The Introduction motivates TTC as an idea "explored for scaling reasoning and generation in other domains" (NLP, video) that "has not been extended to spatio-temporal forecasting." But "generate many stochastic, spatially-coherent candidate fields and then select/calibrate among them" is exactly what ensemble numerical weather prediction and, more recently, generative/diffusion ensemble weather models (e.g., GenCast, already cited in Sec. 2.5 for a different point) already do at scale, together with a long tradition of ensemble post-processing (EMOS, rank histograms, best-member/analog selection). None of this is acknowledged. This creates real risk of an implicit overclaim ("TTC... has not been extended to spatio-temporal forecasting") that a meteorology-literate reviewer will push back on, even if the *specific mechanism* (Best-of-1 selection from a frozen statistical TS-FM via a composite plausibility verifier, rather than ensemble-mean/spread calibration of a dynamical or generative ensemble) is indeed different.
   - *Suggestion*: Add a short paragraph acknowledging ensemble NWP post-processing as prior art for "generate multiple spatially coherent candidates + select/calibrate," and state precisely how ST-TTC differs: it post-processes a *frozen general-purpose time-series foundation model* (not a dedicated dynamical/generative weather ensemble), and it performs discrete *best-candidate selection* via a composite plausibility verifier rather than continuous ensemble calibration. This both closes an obvious gap and lets the "we are first in spatio-temporal TTC" claim stand on firmer ground.

5. **(Moderate) The paper's real differentiator (spatial coherence) is not foregrounded early enough, which could look like a narrowing of the claim after the fact.** The Abstract and the Introduction's contribution list both present accuracy and spatial coherence as co-equal selling points ("improves accuracy... by [X]% RMSE and yields the clearest gains in spatial coherence"). By the Main Results section, the framing has already shifted to spatial coherence being "the clearest advantage," with accuracy gains described more modestly ("remains competitive," "attains best or comparable"). If, once real numbers are in, RMSE gains turn out to be small while Moran's $I$ gains are large (which the current ablation/ablation-table structure — pairing RMSE with Moran's $I$ everywhere — seems to anticipate), the Abstract/Intro framing will look like it oversold the accuracy angle relative to what the paper actually delivers.
   - *Suggestion*: State the central claim precisely and consistently from the Abstract onward: "spatial coherence improves substantially, with accuracy at least maintained (not necessarily improved) relative to single-pass inference," rather than listing RMSE improvement and spatial-coherence improvement as two independent, equally strong claims.

6. **(Minor) The "Train? = No" label for "Ours" in Table main_results elides the use of a validation split.** Section 4.1 states the verifier weights $w_1,w_2,w_3$ and the climatology used for ACC are "computed on a disjoint validation period." This is a defensible and common practice (it is not backbone fine-tuning), but a bare "No" in the Train? column, positioned to visually equate ST-TTC's training status with PINFDiT's, could be read as implying zero use of held-out data for calibration.
   - *Suggestion*: Add a footnote to Table main_results clarifying that "No" means no gradient update to the backbone $\theta$; verifier weights and the physics-filter threshold are selected on a small disjoint validation split rather than a dedicated training set.

7. **(Minor) Graph-based related work (Sec. 2.5) anchors only to GraphCast/GenCast, not to the literature that actually originated the specific technique used (graph-Laplacian smoothness as a consistency score).** GraphCast/GenCast are trained end-to-end GNN *architectures* for prediction; the paper's own spatial-consistency term is a graph-signal-processing smoothness functional used as a *scoring criterion*, which is a different lineage (closer to the spectral graph theory already cited via `vonluxburg2007tutorial`/`ng2002spectral`, and to the broader non-weather spatio-temporal GNN forecasting literature that uses Laplacian regularization as a training-time penalty).
   - *Suggestion*: Add 1–2 references for graph-Laplacian-smoothness-as-regularizer in spatio-temporal forecasting (outside weather) to correctly anchor where the *scoring idea* comes from, separate from where the architecture idea (GraphCast-style message passing) comes from.

8. **(Minor) Internal inconsistency between Table fm_comparison and the "Backbone selection" narrative.** Table 1 lists TimesFM's context length as "[512]" and its univariate/multivariate column as "[TBD]," yet the adjacent prose already asserts a specific, non-trivial fact about it ("native multivariate support in TimesFM was introduced only in its more recent versions"). The narrative claim and the table placeholder need to be reconciled with the same TimesFM version once filled in; Table ablation_backbones separately lists both "TimesFM" and "TimesFM-3" without explaining the difference.
   - *Suggestion*: Fix one canonical TimesFM version/checkpoint identifier and use it consistently across Table 1, the Backbone-selection paragraph, and Table ablation_backbones.

9. **(Minor) The Conclusion's "best or comparable... among training-free methods" claim reduces to an n=1, non-controlled comparison.** By the paper's own accounting, PINFDiT is the *only* other training-free baseline in Table main_results, and its numbers are as-reported under a different setup (already flagged in the table caption). Stating this as a settled finding in the Conclusion is stronger language than the underlying comparison supports.
   - *Suggestion*: Either carry out the already-flagged TODO (re-running PINFDiT, and ideally DEFT, under the paper's own controlled ERA5 setup) or soften the Conclusion's phrasing to re-state the as-reported caveat rather than presenting it as a resolved empirical ranking.

## Questions for Authors

1. Have you now read SCALER (arXiv 2608.08675) in full? If so, does its coarse-shape model require per-dataset training, and does this change how you describe the delta from ST-TTC's coarse branch (which reuses the frozen backbone with zero extra training)?
2. Can you confirm, from PINFDiT's actual experiments, whether its physics-guided Langevin correction is computed independently per channel in its multivariate setting, or whether it already incorporates some form of cross-channel/cross-series coupling? This directly determines whether "does not model interactions between grid points" is accurate or needs qualification.
3. DEFT's "expert evaluator" — is it ever instantiated, in the DEFT paper's own experiments, as something resembling an automatic/free signal (e.g., a cheap heuristic standing in for a human expert), or is it always a genuinely costly oracle? This affects how strongly you can claim that "needing no costly oracle" is a differentiator.
4. Given that the clustering is computed once from static grid geometry (Sec. 3.2) and is explicitly flagged in the Conclusion as a limitation, do you expect the spatial-coherence gain (Moran's $I$) to be sensitive to the choice of $M$ in a way that could make the headline spatial-coherence claim fragile to this hyperparameter? The current ablation (Table ablation_clustering) varies the clustering *algorithm* at fixed $M$ but, as far as I can tell, does not vary $M$ itself.

## Recommendation

**Major Revision**

The core idea — a frozen-backbone, two-scale (coarse-cluster / fine-grid) Best-of-$N$ framework with a physics pre-filter and a composite trend/frequency/spatial verifier — is a legitimate and reasonably specific contribution to test-time computing for spatio-temporal forecasting, and the paper's self-awareness about its own limitations (as-reported baselines, soft physics constraint, single dataset) is a genuine strength. However, direct verification of the three closest-work citations (DEFT, SCALER, PINFDiT) during this review surfaced at least two likely mischaracterizations (DEFT's costly-oracle verifier described as if it were free and autonomous; PINFDiT's scope described as single-series despite its own reported multivariate experiments) and one structurally important, currently unacknowledged similarity (SCALER's coarse-anchor-then-refine mechanism). Combined with the complete absence of ensemble-NWP post-processing from the related-work discussion, the novelty argument as currently written is not yet reliable, even though the underlying contribution plausibly survives a corrected framing. I would upgrade to Minor Revision / Accept once (a) the DEFT and PINFDiT characterizations are corrected or substantiated with direct quotes/citations from those papers, (b) the SCALER comparison is sharpened on mechanism (trained coarse model + active guidance vs. frozen-backbone reuse + post-hoc verification) rather than only on domain (single series vs. spatial), and (c) a short acknowledgment of ensemble weather post-processing as adjacent prior art is added.
